## Learned
- **`self_checked, high confidence`**: no reward head exists anywhere in the tree —
  `src/model/decoder.ts:20`'s doc comment ("the decoder (and reward/continuation heads, not built
  yet)") and `docs/explainers/0011`'s "what's deliberately not here" section ("Reward head ...
  Still out of scope") both confirm it directly. This is a hard blocker for actually running
  Dreamer-style imagination training (`computeLambdaReturns`'s `rewards` array has no source once
  a rollout leaves real environment steps), so I flagged it prominently in `0013` as a
  prerequisite this spec deliberately does not design — it's a world-model addition, not an
  actor-critic one, and out of a docs-only actor-critic increment's scope.
- **`self_checked, high confidence`**: `ReplayBufferEntry` (`0012`) cannot supply a valid
  imagination-rollout starting state on its own — it stores a flat `Transition`
  (`observation`/`action`/`reward`/`nextObservation`/`done`) with no accompanying `RSSMState`, and
  `RSSMState` is a hidden function of an entire trajectory prefix (`RSSMCell.step`'s recurrence),
  not recoverable from one sampled transition's `observation` field. `0012` never claimed replay
  transitions *are* the imagination seed (its own text: "no consumer in the tree yet"), so this
  isn't a bug in `0012` — but it's a real design tension a first-time actor-critic implementer
  could easily miss. `0013` resolves it by proposing imagination start from
  `WorldModel.currentState` directly (already tracked in-process during a real rollout) rather
  than through `ReplayBuffer.sample()`, without proposing any change to `ReplayBufferEntry`'s
  shape — keeping `ReplayBuffer` scoped to what `0012` already specified it for (ordinary/
  recency-weighted experience replay), per the review's "don't redesign them" instruction.
- **`self_checked, high confidence`**: the gradient-separation invariant reuses a pattern already
  live in this codebase (`WorldModel.step()`'s explicit `tf.variableGrads(forward, varList)`,
  `worldModel.ts:239`, plus its own comment on why an implicit varList is unsafe here,
  `worldModel.ts:120`) rather than needing a new mechanism — the same explicit-varList discipline
  that protects world-model training from picking up unrelated variables also protects
  actor-critic training from picking up world-model variables, just pointed the other way.
