## Learned

- **[medium, self_checked, execution-confirmed]** Checking all gradient components instead of a
  fixed cap of 3 is cheap at this model's current scale (tens to low hundreds of elements per
  variable) — the two affected tests together cost well under 1.5s of the ~2.3s suite total. This
  won't hold indefinitely: Arm-A dims (`h=256`, 8×4 categorical latent per `experiments/2026-07-
  21-week3-stack-spike/`) would put `kernel`-sized variables in the tens of thousands of elements,
  at which point full-coverage finite-differencing (two forward passes per element) stops being
  free. Worth revisiting the cap-vs-full tradeoff (or switching to a random-subset check with a
  fixed seed) before this helper is reused at full model scale, rather than assuming today's "it's
  free" verdict generalizes.
- **[low, self_checked]** My independent re-measurement of the full-135-element max error
  (`4.91e-4`) is close to but not identical to the reviewer's own figure (`5.87e-4`) for what should
  be the same computation. Not investigated further — both are well inside the `5e-3` tolerance and
  the qualitative conclusion (ample margin) is unaffected either way — but worth noting as a minor
  unexplained discrepancy rather than silently treating the two numbers as interchangeable; likely
  candidates are float32 nondeterminism across processes or a small difference in perturbation
  order, neither of which would change the pass/fail outcome.
