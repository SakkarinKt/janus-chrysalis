## Learned

- **The "underpowered pre-freeze horizon" hypothesis (2026-08-12/2026-08-13 updates) is likely
  wrong, not just unconfirmed** (`self_checked, high confidence` on the raw counts; `medium
  confidence` on this reading). All 3 seeds show 22–23 of 38 post-freeze steps (58–61%) with
  control and intervention picking *different* joint actions — the instrument has real power at
  this horizon. The hypothesis predicted the opposite: that 37 pre-freeze steps was too little
  training for post-freeze exploration to ever land on a state where the two conditions would
  diverge at all.
- **Seed 1003's exactly-zero `diffMean` was misread in the 2026-08-13 stand-up.** That stand-up
  said "agent 1's post-freeze actions never diverged between conditions... read directly off the
  equal telemetry" — but "equal loss series" does not imply "equal actions," and today's direct
  count shows seed 1003 actually had the *most* divergent actions of the three seeds (23/38), not
  the fewest. The earlier claim was an unverified inference, not a measurement — this run replaces
  it with one.
- **Net effect: the open question changes shape.** It's not "did the post-freeze window give the
  conditions a chance to diverge" (yes, clearly) — it's "why does the frozen agent's world-model
  loss not track that behavioral divergence into a consistent, rising, cross-seed signal." That's
  a different, and arguably more interesting, question than the one the multi-episode
  training-then-freeze follow-up (PR #46's "Next") was designed to answer.
